The findings that matter, in order

1 · A product that Ghana buyers are told to buy in a version that can't be bought

The flame-and-jellyfish aroma diffuser says, in its own description: "For Ghanaian wall sockets, choose the UK plug variant." Every UK-plug variant is unavailable. Only 2 of its 26 variants can be bought, both with EU plugs, at GH₵2,145 and above. A buyer who follows the instructions hits a dead end.

Fix: check supplier stock for the UK-plug SKUs. If they're gone, hide the product or rewrite the note to say an EU-to-UK adapter is needed. Then run a real cart test.

2 · A delivery promise that asks a lot of a local buyer

"10–50 days within Ghana", with duties payable on arrival, appears on 22 product pages and in the policy. It's the honest carrier range, and the store should keep being honest about it. But 23 people reached checkout and one paid. This is the most likely reason, and no theme change can fix it.

Fix: this is a fulfilment decision, not a design one. Options: stock the top 3 sellers locally, test a faster shipping line on those 3, or show a realistic typical range next to the maximum. Measure checkout completion before and after.

3 · Prices and names that look unfinished

186 variants end in odd pesewas like GH₵358.64, supplier typos show in variant names, and the catalog has one near-duplicate product. None of these blocks a purchase. Together they make a store look less cared for at the moment someone decides whether to trust it with their MoMo.

Fix: round prices to the store's own pattern (a margin check on each), clean the variant names, and merge or remove the duplicate.

4 · Search is small but converts; social is big but doesn't

84 search sessions produced 10 cart adds. 2,486 social sessions produced 17. The 70 hashed alt texts and 16 default SEO titles are cheap to fix, and they sit on the only channel that's already converting.

Fix: descriptive alt text and custom SEO titles on all 66 products. Then track search sessions monthly, without expecting overnight results.

What the audit ruled out

Just as useful: nine common "zero sales" causes were checked and cleared, so no time goes into fixing things that aren't broken. The storefront is open. Currency and gateway match. A real payment went through this week. Cart and checkout load. Every market has a shipping rate. The policy matches checkout. The discount it advertises is real. The payment badges are honest. Every product is categorised.

What this case study doesn't claim

  • No fixes had been applied when this audit was written (22 Sep 2026). There's no before/after result yet. If one comes, it will be added with its date, including if nothing changes.
  • No promise that fixing these raises sales. The audit finds what's blocking buyers; buyers still have to want the product.
  • The delivery-time finding is a hypothesis based on the funnel, not a tested result.

How the audit was done

  • Live data, not settings screens: Shopify analytics for the funnel, the Admin API for products, Markets, shipping zones and discounts, and the public storefront for what a shopper actually sees.
  • A real test cart on a mobile browser, through to the checkout page, then cleared.
  • A full variant sweep of all 527 variants, because one unbuyable default can hide behind a clean-looking product page.
  • Every finding checked twice: the data was pulled from several sources and cross-checked, and each finding was confirmed by hand on the live store before it went in.
